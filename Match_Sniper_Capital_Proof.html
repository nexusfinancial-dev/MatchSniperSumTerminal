Logarithmic Derivation of the 1% Risk Threshold:

A complete sequence collapse requires n consecutive trade failures. Assuming mutual independence of tick digit arrivals:

P(\text{Sequence Failure}) = q^n = (0.80)^n

We formulate the risk boundary condition that failure probability must strictly fall below 1% (0.01):

(0.80)^n < 0.01

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides:

\ln\left((0.80)^n\right) < \ln(0.01) \implies n \cdot \ln(0.80) < \ln(0.01)

Because ln(0.80) ≈ -0.22314355 is negative, dividing by it reverses the inequality:

n > \frac{\ln(0.01)}{\ln(0.80)} = \frac{-4.60517019}{-0.22314355} \approx 20.638

Because the trial count n must be an integer:
n ≥ 21 steps.

Evaluating at n = 21:

P(\text{Loss})_{21} = (0.80)^{21} = 0.00922337 = \mathbf{0.922\%} < \mathbf{1.0\%}
P(\text{Win})_{21} = 1 - (0.80)^{21} = 0.99077663 = \mathbf{99.078\%} \approx \mathbf{99.1\%}
